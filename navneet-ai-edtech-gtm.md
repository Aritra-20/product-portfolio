# AI EdTech in India — PMF Validation & Go-To-Market

**Status:** in progress · empirical study, PGPM (Product Management)

## Context

Navneet Education has published learning material in India for 65 years. It has built its own AI model, Navneet LM, and already launched a B2B product for teachers. The next move is direct to consumer: students and parents.

This project sits between product management and marketing strategy. It looks at four candidate B2C products:

| Product idea | What it is |
|---|---|
| AI tutor | A Socratic tutoring engine that guides students to answers instead of handing them over |
| Exam Center | Board-exam style assessments and practice |
| Skill Labs | Co-certified skill sandboxes |
| Phygital device | A screen-free, vernacular learning device for young children |

## Research questions

1. **Product-market fit:** do these products actually meet what students, parents and educators need?
2. **Product shape:** given how global EdTech products are built and what domestic competitors offer, what's the right feature set, packaging and positioning?
3. **Go-to-market:** what pricing, acquisition plan and launch strategy will get people to adopt the products and keep using them?

## Method

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. User & market validation** | Interviews and surveys with students, parents and school owners/teachers; a scan of global EdTech trends; competitive intelligence on domestic and international players |
| **2. Prototyping & usability testing** | Build a prototype of one high-value workflow with product engineering (likely the Exam Center assessment flow or the Socratic tutor), then run structured usability sessions on interaction, content quality and satisfaction |
| **3. GTM & marketing blueprint** | Focus groups to pressure-test the value proposition and surface real adoption drivers (trust, perceived learning value, parental worries about screen time), then turn that into recommendations on packaging, pricing, distribution and promotion |

## Why it's interesting

Most EdTech GTM plans assume the student is the customer. In Indian K-12, the parent pays, the student uses, and the school often decides whether to trust it. So the study treats **trust and parental concern as adoption drivers to design around**, not objections to handle after launch.
